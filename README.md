## Three things that can't be undone

**1. Buying the wrong amount.** No listing accepts returns on gift cards, and once a code is redeemed the balance is spent. If you're buying for someone whose taste you're guessing at, a smaller denomination plus cash is more forgiving than a $500 code they'll use on one pair of AirPods and forget about.

**2. Buying for someone outside the US.** The terms are explicit: "Valid only for U.S. transactions in Apple properties." A US card will not work on a UK, Canadian or UAE Apple Account. One reviewer on a large retail listing described sending several cards to her fiancé stationed overseas with the Army; several came up "empty" and unusable, and with no refunds available she was out a lot of money. That's one buyer's report, not proof of a systematic failure, but it shows what happens when a region mismatch meets a no-refund policy.

**3. Getting a code that's empty or short.** This is where the low ratings come from. The $500 eGift listing sits at 4.1 out of 5 across 54,971 ratings, and **19% of those ratings are one star** against 72% at five stars. The Walmart listing for the same $15–$500 card scores 3.7 out of 5 across 3,926 ratings with only 52% recommending it; 969 of those ratings are one star, roughly a quarter. Amazon's own review summary says buyers find the card easy and fast to use and good for sending money to family, while functionality and value draw mixed feedback, with some reporting it "doesn't work." On the Walmart side, one buyer said a friend redeemed a card and "only received $50." Another called the cards a waste of money after they arrived unusable.

The complaints cluster around redemption codes, not around what the card buys. That distinction matters when you're deciding how much to put on one card: a single $500 code is one point of failure, while five $100 codes spread the risk, and the terms don't stop you from redeeming several cards to the same account.

## Redeeming the card and the warning printed on the wrapper

Redemption takes about a minute. Go to apple.com/redeem, sign in with the Apple Account you want the balance on, and enter the 16-digit code from the email. The balance then shows in your Apple Account and, on iPhone, on the Apple Account card in Wallet. Every card is issued by **Apple Value Services, LLC**, and support runs through support.apple.com/giftcard or 1-800-275-2273.

Every listing also carries the same warning, and it's worth repeating because gift card fraud is the one scenario where you lose the entire $500 with no recourse: **"Beware of gift card scams. Do not share your code."** Gift cards are for gifts, not emergency payments. Anyone who asks you to pay a bill, a fine, a tax debt or a "verification fee" with an Apple gift card is running a scam, and the same goes for anyone asking you to read out the code. Reports go to ReportFraud.ftc.gov or your state attorney general's consumer fraud division. Apple states plainly that it isn't responsible for unauthorized use, which means the code is as sensitive as cash.

## FAQ

**Is $500 really the maximum?**
On the US listings that sell the full range, yes. The range is described as $15–$500, and $500 is the top of it. Some sellers only stock $25, $50 and $100, and one discount chain starts at $10 instead of $15.

**Does it expire?**
No expiration is listed. The product spec reads Has expiration: N, and the download code field is dated 9999-12-31. Redeemed balances sit in your Apple Account rather than on the card.

**Can I return it if I buy the wrong amount?**
No. "No returns or refunds on Apple Gift Cards" appears on every listing, and the Walmart product field simply says Not Returnable. Check the amount before you pay, because there's no correction afterward.

**Can I send it to someone in another country?**
Not usefully. The card is valid only for US transactions in Apple properties. A 500 AED card sold in the UAE carries the same number but a different currency and a different region, and neither card works in the other's store.

**Can I buy an iPhone with it?**
Yes, at an Apple Store, in the Apple Store app or on apple.com, alongside accessories, apps and subscriptions. A $500 balance won't cover a Pro iPhone on its own, so you'd pay the difference with another method. Some purchases may still ask for a card on file even when the balance covers the total.

**What if the code doesn't work?**
Contact Apple support through support.apple.com/giftcard or 1-800-275-2273 with the order details. Keep the original email; since the sale is non-returnable, the purchase record is the only thing that can support a resolution.

## Which Apple Gift Card to buy

Buying for yourself, to offset a hardware purchase or prepay subscriptions: pick the amount you'll actually spend in the next few months. There's no discount for going bigger, and a $500 balance locked in an Apple Account is worse than $500 in a savings account if the purchase slips.

Buying for someone in the US: the digital card is the fastest option and the one with SMS delivery if their inbox is unreliable. If the gift is for a teenager or a new iPhone owner, $100 to $200 usually lands better than $500, and it's easier to spread across several codes.

Buying for someone outside the US: don't buy a US card. It won't redeem, and there's no refund window to fall back on.

Two things to confirm at checkout, both irreversible: the recipient's email address or phone number, and the denomination. A mistyped address sends $500 to a stranger, and the terms put unauthorized use on you.
